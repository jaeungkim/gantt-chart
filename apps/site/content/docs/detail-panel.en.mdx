A bar carries a name, two dates and a progress fill. Every other field you hold about that task has
nowhere to go. The detail panel is a pane docked to the right of the chart that shows one task at a
time. It is a sibling of the timeline rather than a layer over it, so the timeline gets narrower
while the panel is open instead of being covered by it, and the chart behind it stays interactive:
this is not a modal, nothing is trapped inside it, and no backdrop swallows the next click.

## Turning the panel on

The panel is off by default. `showDetail` turns it on. When `showDetail` is omitted, passing
`renderDetail` turns it on for you — the renderer is the switch, the way `showTaskList` is the
switch for the pane in [Task list and hierarchy](task-list).

| `showDetail` | `renderDetail` | Panel | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| omitted | omitted | hidden | — |
| omitted | given | shown | yours |
| `true` | omitted | shown | the built-in body |
| `true` | given | shown | yours |
| `false` | omitted | hidden | — |
| `false` | given | hidden | — |

An explicit `false` wins over `renderDetail`.

Turning the panel on also turns row selection on, so the row whose detail is open is the highlighted
one. An explicit `selectable={false}` still wins: the panel opens and closes as usual, nothing is
highlighted. What selection is and which callbacks it fires is in [Events](events). The signature
and default of every prop named here are in [GanttProps](ref/props).

## What the default body renders

With the panel on and no `renderDetail`, the built-in body is `task.name` at the top — a text input
unless `readOnly` is set on the task or on the chart — over a short field list.

| Caption | Value | Shown | Takes input when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start, End | the dates, through the current scale's tooltip formatter — the one the hover tooltip uses, so `locale` and `formats` already control them | always | `allowResize` leaves the task resizable |
| Duration | the span between them — whole hours under a day, whole days from there — the same readout the hover tooltip prints | always | never — it is derived |
| Progress | `task.progress`, clamped to 0–100 | when the task carries one | `allowProgressChange` leaves the progress handle draggable |
| Depends on | the names of the tasks its `dependencies` point at; an id naming no task prints as the raw id | when it has any | never — it is derived |

Inside one of those inputs, `Escape` reverts that field and leaves the panel open.

That is the whole default. It shows no other field of the task, and there is no prop that adds a
row: a field needs a caption, and only the host knows what that caption should say. The captions
above are fixed English strings, like the accessible names of the panel and its close button, and
there is no prop that translates them. A host that must localize them, or wants any other field,
renders the body itself with `renderDetail`, below.

## Editing in place

The built-in body is editable exactly where the task's interaction flags already allow the matching
gesture — the panel adds no flag of its own. The name is a text input unless the task, or failing
that the chart, is `readOnly`; no narrower flag exists for the name. Start and End are date inputs
when the task can be resized, because changing one date *is* a resize — which is why a summary row's
dates stay text. Progress is a number input when the progress handle would be draggable. How those
flags resolve, task against chart, is in [Editing tasks](editing). Duration and Depends on are
derived, and stay text for everyone.

A field commits on blur and on `Enter`, and the change arrives through `onTasksChange` like every
drag — same array, same shape, no separate callback. `Escape` reverts the field without closing the
panel. A value that cannot commit reverts silently — a blank name, an incomplete date, an
end that would not land after the start, an emptied progress field — nothing flashes red.

Editing a date replaces the date part only: the task keeps its time-of-day, and the result is
clamped to the same `minDate`/`maxDate` a drag obeys. Progress is clamped to 0–100. While the dates
are editable, the native date input shows the plain `YYYY-MM-DD` in place of the formatted text —
the tooltip formatting above comes back wherever resizing is off.

## What opens it

A single click on a bar or on a task-list row, and `Enter` or `Space` on the focused row — unless
the row is expandable, where those keys toggle it instead. That is the whole gesture set: there is
no double-click mode and no prop that picks another trigger.

A single click, because a double click is the convention of the *modal* task editors: Bryntum's
TaskEdit defaults to `triggerEvent: 'eventdblclick'`, DHTMLX opens its lightbox on `dblclick`. Those
open a form you have to finish or cancel, so they are expensive and ask for a deliberate gesture. A
docked panel is cheap in both directions — it opens on the click that already selects the row and
closes with `Escape` — and frappe-gantt's `popup_trigger` does not even offer a double click, only
`'click'` and `'hover'`.

`onTaskClick` still fires for that click — first, as always — and the panel opens after it. Clicking
the row whose panel is already open changes nothing. To let only your own code open it, hold
`detailTaskId` yourself, as in the controlled mode below: a click then only reports through
`onDetailChange`, and you decide whether to follow it.

The panel is an `<aside role="complementary" aria-label="Task details">`, not a dialog: no focus
trap, no backdrop. It sits outside the treegrid, so the chart keeps exactly one tab stop and the
panel is the next one. `Escape` closes it, and the listener is on the document, so it works with
focus anywhere on the page — and it does not swallow the key, so the host's own `Escape` handlers
still run. Closing puts focus back where it was when the panel opened, unless focus has since moved
elsewhere on the page — the panel never pulls it away from a field the user has moved on to. The
key map gains no new key for any of this — `Enter` on a focused row already announced the row and
now opens the panel too. Every key is listed in [Keyboard and screen readers](accessibility).

## Controlled and uncontrolled

The open task is one id, in either mode.

| Prop | Mode | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `detailTaskId` | controlled | a `string` or `null` here is what the chart shows, and internal state stops updating |
| `detailTaskId` omitted | uncontrolled | the chart holds the open task itself |
| `onDetailChange` | both | fires on every open and close, with the task or `null` |

The mode switch is `detailTaskId !== undefined`. `null` is not an opt-out the way it is elsewhere:
it is the controlled way to say "closed", which is why the chart tests against `undefined` instead
of falling back with `??`.

> [!WARNING]
> In controlled mode the chart opens nothing on its own. It waits for a new `detailTaskId`. Pass
> `detailTaskId` without handling `onDetailChange` and every click looks ignored.

`onDetailChange` fires in controlled and uncontrolled mode alike, and only when the open task
actually changes: clicking the row that is already open fires nothing. It never fires on mount, and
it is not a change event for the panel's contents — moving a bar whose detail is open does not fire
it.

## Opening it from your own code

`GanttHandle` carries `openDetail(taskId)` and `closeDetail()`; an unknown id is ignored the way
`scrollToTask` ignores one, and both are no-ops while the panel is off. In controlled mode they
follow the same rule as a click: they only report through `onDetailChange`, and your new
`detailTaskId` is what opens the panel. They are listed with the rest of the ref in
[GanttHandle](ref/handle).

## Replacing the body

`renderDetail` replaces everything inside the panel. The `<aside>` itself stays the chart's — its
width, its `role`, its accessible name and the focus handling on close are not yours to lose. Only
the children change, which also means the close button is yours to render: `close` is the same
function the built-in button calls, and `Escape` keeps working whether you render one or not.

The renderer receives a `GanttDetailRenderProps`: the open `task` as the chart transformed it,
`close`, the current `scale`, and `update`. `update(patch)` patches the open task through the same
commit path as every gesture — the store, then `onTasksChange` — so a custom form can save without
holding the array itself. It checks no interaction flags: the gates above belong to the built-in
body, and a custom body enforces its own rules. The type is spelled out in
[Detail renderer](ref/renderers).

```tsx title="ProjectChart.tsx"
import { useState } from 'react';
import { ReactGanttChart } from '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart';
import type { GanttDetailRenderProps, Task } from '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart';
import '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart/style.css';

const tasks: Task[] = [
  { id: 'p1', name: 'Phase 1', parentId: null, sequence: '1',
    startDate: '2025-03-03', endDate: '2025-03-28' },
  { id: 't1', name: 'Design', parentId: 'p1', sequence: '1.1',
    startDate: '2025-03-03', endDate: '2025-03-12', progress: 100 },
  { id: 't2', name: 'Build', parentId: 'p1', sequence: '1.2',
    startDate: '2025-03-12', endDate: '2025-03-28', progress: 40 },
];

function TaskDetail({ task, close }: GanttDetailRenderProps) {
  return (
    <>
      <div style={{ display: 'flex', justifyContent: 'space-between', gap: 8 }}>
        <h2 style={{ margin: 0, fontSize: 14 }}>{task.name}</h2>
        <button type="button" onClick={close} aria-label="Close task details">
          ✕
        </button>
      </div>
      <p style={{ margin: 0, fontSize: 12 }}>
        {task.startDate.slice(0, 10)} → {task.endDate.slice(0, 10)}
      </p>
      <p style={{ margin: 0, fontSize: 12 }}>{task.progress ?? 0}% done</p>
    </>
  );
}

export function ProjectChart() {
  const [detailTaskId, setDetailTaskId] = useState<string | null>(null);

  return (
    <ReactGanttChart
      tasks={tasks}
      showTaskList
      hierarchy
      renderDetail={(props) => <TaskDetail {...props} />}
      detailTaskId={detailTaskId}
      onDetailChange={(task) => setDetailTaskId(task?.id ?? null)}
      height={420}
    />
  );
}
```

`renderDetail` is the chart's only render prop. Nothing else in the chart is replaceable — a bar's
colour and class name are fields on the task, and they are in [Styling](styling).

## Panel width

The panel's width is the custom property `--gantt-detail-width`, listed with its default among the
rest of the tokens in [Theming](theming). It is laid out as `flex: 0 0` on that value, so it neither
grows nor shrinks: the timeline absorbs the whole change.

## Edge cases

**The open task leaves `tasks`.** The panel closes with it, because the open id no longer resolves to
a task. `onDetailChange` is deliberately *not* fired for that: your own change to `tasks` caused it,
so it is not news to you.

**`detailTaskId` naming a task that is not in the array.** Same result — the panel is closed, and no
error is raised. It opens as soon as an id that does resolve arrives.

**`Escape` leaves the row selected.** It closes the panel only. `onTaskSelect` does not fire.

**Clicking empty timeline space.** The selection is cleared *and* the panel closes.

**Collapsing the open task's parent.** The panel stays open. Ids resolve against every task the chart
holds, not against the visible rows, so a hidden row keeps its panel.

**Clicking another row while the panel is open.** The panel switches to that task in place and does
not pull focus back — focus is restored only when the panel unmounts.

**The panel answers to the click, not to the selection changing.** That is
deliberate. Selection ignores a click on the already-selected row, so a panel driven by the selection
would stay shut after `Escape` however often you clicked the same row.

## Limits

The panel does not resize. There is no drag handle and no width prop — `--gantt-detail-width` is the
only lever, and it is CSS, so it cannot be animated into place by a host that wants a spring.

Placement is fixed on the right. There is no left, bottom or floating variant, and no prop to detach
it into a window of its own.

It shows one task at a time. There are no tabs, no history, no back button between tasks, and no
multi-select panel.

Editing stops at the fields the panel shows. Duration and Depends on are never inputs, no prop adds
a row, and a task field the built-in body does not list cannot be edited there — a richer form is
your `renderDetail`, committing through `update`.

The slide is not a lever. The panel opens and closes by transitioning its `flex-basis` over 200ms,
the timeline reflowing with it, and there is no prop to retime or disable that;
`prefers-reduced-motion` collapses it to instant, like every other transition in the chart.
Switching tasks while the panel is open swaps the body in place, with no slide.

So the host app owns: any control that toggles `showDetail`, holding the open id in state if it wants
controlled mode, everything a form in `renderDetail` does, and persisting the panel width if a user
should be able to change it.
