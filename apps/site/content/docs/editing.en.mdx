Four pointer gestures on a bar write task data: move, resize left, resize right and progress. This
page covers those gestures, the permission flags, drag bounds, touch and auto-scrolling. Drawing a
range below the last row is described in [Creating tasks](task-creation).
[Reordering rows](reordering) covers dragging a row in the task list.

## Gestures

| Gesture | Start | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| Move | press on the bar, outside the edge zones | `startDate` and `endDate` |
| Resize left | press in the bar's left edge zone | `startDate` |
| Resize right | press in the bar's right edge zone | `endDate` |
| Progress | press on the progress handle | `progress` |

All four are on by default. The minimum setup is a `tasks` array and an `onTasksChange` handler.

```tsx title="src/App.tsx"
import { useState } from 'react';
import { ReactGanttChart, type Task } from '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart';
import '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart/style.css';

const initial: Task[] = [
  {
    id: 'design',
    name: 'Design',
    startDate: '2026-03-02T00:00:00Z',
    endDate: '2026-03-06T00:00:00Z',
    parentId: null,
    sequence: '1',
    progress: 40,
  },
  {
    id: 'build',
    name: 'Build',
    startDate: '2026-03-09T00:00:00Z',
    endDate: '2026-03-20T00:00:00Z',
    parentId: null,
    sequence: '2',
    progress: 0,
  },
];

export function App() {
  const [tasks, setTasks] = useState<Task[]>(initial);
  return <ReactGanttChart tasks={tasks} onTasksChange={setTasks} />;
}
```

A committed gesture produces one `onTasksChange` call with the full array, even when it moved a
whole subtree. A gesture that does not travel far enough to move the bar commits nothing and calls
nothing.

The array does not change while the pointer is down. The moving bar is a pixel offset kept apart
from the task data, and the array changes on release. The drag readout shows the date each moving
edge has reached, in the tick cell above it. [Locale and date formats](i18n) covers its format.

### Edge zones

The edge zone is 8px wide for a mouse and 44px for touch or pen. A pen counts as touch throughout
this page.

A mouse always has the zones, because a bar is never rendered narrower than 24px and 24px is the
minimum a mouse needs. Touch and pen need 132px of rendered width. Below that the whole bar is the
move handle and there is no resize zone. 132px is 33 days at the quarter scale and 132 days at the
year scale. A task loses its touch resize edges well before it looks small.

The edge zones are tested before the move zone. A task with `allowMove: false` and
`allowResize: true` resizes at its edges and does nothing in the middle.

### Progress drag

The percentage is the pointer's position across the bar's rendered rectangle, clamped to 0 to 100
and rounded to a whole percent. There is no step size on the pointer path.

The value is sampled on pointer movement only. Pressing the handle and releasing without moving
changes nothing.

Cancellation differs between the two kinds of gesture. A progress drag that the browser cancels
commits its last value. A bar move or resize cancelled the same way reverts and writes nothing.

### Drag threshold

A drag commits nothing until the pointer has travelled half a drag step. Below that the gesture
ends without a write, and the trailing click reaches `onTaskClick`. After the bar has moved by a
step, the click that ends the drag is not delivered. See [Events](events) for the click callbacks.

## Drag steps

A drag does not snap to an absolute grid line. The chart rounds the pointer's travel to a whole
number of drag steps and adds that many steps to the task's original dates.

A task that starts at 09:37 starts at 09:37 after a four-day drag on the month scale. The time of
day survives every drag, and no bar is aligned to midnight or to a tick boundary.

Rounding the step count moves the bar to the next step at half a step of pointer travel. The
length of a step and the pointer travel per step differ per scale. See [The timeline](timeline).

The scale is captured when the gesture starts. Changing scale during a drag does not change the
unit of the gesture in progress.

A resize always leaves at least one drag step of width. The step count itself is clamped, not only
the preview, so a resize never commits an end date before its start date. On a bar already narrower
than one step the same clamp works the other way and widens it.

With a working calendar configured, the dragged edge also snaps forward to the next working day. A
move snaps both ends together and the bar keeps its length. See [Working calendar](working-calendar).

## Permissions

Move, resize, progress and the two dependency link flags resolve per task through four levels.
`allowReorder` resolves through a shorter chain whose fallback is `false`. Only an explicit flag
turns it on. `allowTaskCreate` applies to the whole chart and has no level per task.
[GanttInteractionConfig](ref/interaction-config) has the full order.

Two results follow from that order. A task's `readOnly: true` overrides a permissive `allowMove`
prop on the chart. A task's own `allowMove: true` overrides both `readOnly` settings, its own and
the chart's.

`minDate` and `maxDate` resolve through a shorter chain of their own, the task's value if it has one
and otherwise the chart's. The two ends resolve independently, so a task can take its lower bound
from itself and its upper bound from the chart.

A blocked gesture does not start. The press does nothing, and the grips and handles for that gesture
are hidden. A bar that cannot be resized hides its resize grips, and a bar whose progress is fixed
hides its handle. The progress fill stays visible as a read-only readout. The signature and default
of every prop named here are in [GanttProps](ref/props).

### Summary rows

A summary row is never resizable and its progress is never draggable, regardless of the flags at
either level. The rule and its reason are in [GanttInteractionConfig](ref/interaction-config).

Moving a summary row is allowed and carries its whole subtree. No other gesture writes more than one
task's dates.

Summary rows exist only when `hierarchy` is on. The rule does not apply to a flat chart. See
[Task list and hierarchy](task-list). `readOnly` on a summary row freezes it completely, move
included.

### Task level overrides

`readOnly` on the chart is the lowest level, so a single task can opt back in.

```tsx title="src/ReviewBoard.tsx"
import { ReactGanttChart, type Task } from '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart';
import '@jaeungkim/gantt-chart/style.css';

const tasks: Task[] = [
  {
    id: 'plan',
    name: 'Plan',
    startDate: '2026-03-02T00:00:00Z',
    endDate: '2026-03-06T00:00:00Z',
    parentId: null,
    sequence: '1',
  },
  {
    id: 'review',
    name: 'Review',
    startDate: '2026-03-09T00:00:00Z',
    endDate: '2026-03-13T00:00:00Z',
    parentId: null,
    sequence: '2',
    // the task's own flag - beats the chart's readOnly below
    allowMove: true,
    allowResize: true,
  },
];

export function ReviewBoard({ onChange }: { onChange: (tasks: Task[]) => void }) {
  return <ReactGanttChart tasks={tasks} readOnly onTasksChange={onChange} />;
}
```

The same shape covers the narrower case. A task with `readOnly: true` and
`allowProgressChange: true` cannot be moved or resized, but its progress handle drags.

## Drag bounds

`minDate` and `maxDate` limit a bar to a window. Both take a UTC ISO string, and a string without a
zone is read as UTC: `minDate="2026-03-01"` means midnight UTC, not local midnight.

What happens at the bound depends on the gesture.

- Resize left clamps the start date into the window and keeps it at least one drag step before the
  end date.
- Resize right clamps the end date into the window and keeps it at least one drag step after the
  start date.
- Move shifts both ends together and the bar keeps its length. When the bar is longer than its
  window, `minDate` applies and the bar overhangs `maxDate`.

The pixel offset is re-measured from the clamped dates every frame, so the bar stops on the bound
instead of overshooting and springing back. The date passed to `onTasksChange` is the bound itself.

Moving a summary row applies one shared delta to the whole subtree, limited by the tightest bound
among the moving tasks. A child's `maxDate` therefore stops its parent's drag. The delta never
overshoots the requested direction, so a task already outside its window does not move further out
and is not pulled back inside.

The minimum width of one step is applied after the bound, so a bar whose window has already been
passed stays a valid bar instead of folding over.

The working calendar snap runs before the clamp. A bar held at its bound can end on a non-working
day.

Bounds apply to bar moves and resizes only. The progress drag and the draw gesture do not read them.

## Touch

A mouse press starts a gesture immediately. A touch or pen press must rest on the bar for 400 ms
before the drag starts.

During that wait the finger may drift up to 10px. Drift is horizontal plus vertical distance from
the landing point. A drift of 6px across and 6px down counts as 12px and cancels the press, even
though it is 8.5px on screen.

Until the drag starts, the bar scrolls with the timeline like the rest of the page. A swipe across a
dense chart is a scroll and not a drag. After the drag starts, touch scrolling is suppressed until
the gesture ends.

The drag starts from where the finger is after the wait, not from where it landed. Four things
cancel a pending drag.

| Cause | Typical case |
|---|---|
| Drift past 10px | a swipe, not a press |
| Release before 400 ms | a tap |
| The browser cancelling the pointer | a system gesture taking over |
| The row unmounting | the row scrolled out of view under the finger |

A bar responds only to the primary pointer's left button. A second finger or a right-click is
rejected at the press, so it does not start a gesture or cancel a pending one. The progress handle
does not have that guard.

On a device with no hover state the handle stays visible as a 10px dot with a 44px hit area.

## Auto-scrolling

`autoScrollOnDrag` defaults to `true`. Only the literal `false` turns it off.

Auto-scrolling starts when the pointer comes within 48px of the timeline viewport's left or right
edge during a bar move or resize. The left edge is the timeline's own, past the task list pane, so
the 48px is counted from where the pane ends and not from the window. A pointer over the pane is
past the edge, so the timeline scrolls left at full speed.

The speed ramps linearly from zero at the inner boundary of the zone to 22px per frame at the edge,
where it is capped. On a narrow timeline the zone shrinks to half the viewport width, and the two
zones never overlap.

Each scrolled frame re-applies the move from the last known pointer position. The drag keeps
advancing while the pointer stays at the edge.

When the gesture is discarded, the auto-scroll is undone and the viewport scrolls smoothly back to
where it started. That covers a cancelled drag and a drop that never travelled a whole step. A
committed drop leaves the viewport where it scrolled to.

Bar moves and resizes auto-scroll. The progress drag and the draw gesture do not.

## Editing from the detail panel

The detail panel's built-in fields take typed values instead of a drag, for an edit where the date
is already known. A committed field goes through the same single `onTasksChange` call and the same
permission levels as the gestures above. A typed date counts as a resize, so a summary row's dates
stay plain text. [Detail panel](detail-panel) describes which fields take input and what an invalid
value does.

## Limitations

Pointer editing writes dates and progress on existing tasks. Everything else is on another page or
is the host app's job.

- Vertical dragging is not a bar gesture. A bar stays in its row, and the row grip in the task list
  moves it. See [Reordering rows](reordering).
- Multiple selection is not supported. One bar is grabbed at a time, and only a summary row moves
  more than one task.
- Midnight and tick snapping are not supported. Drags are relative step shifts that preserve the
  time of day. The working calendar snap shifts by whole days and also leaves the time of day alone.
- Persistence is the host app's job. `onTasksChange` passes an array, and the host app passes it
  back as the `tasks` prop. The chart stores nothing and sends nothing.
- Validation is the host app's job. The chart enforces its own bounds and its minimum bar length and
  nothing else. Business rules belong in the `onTasksChange` handler.
- Dependency arrows are a separate gesture with their own permission flags. See
  [Dependencies](dependencies).
- A bar shorter than one drag step grows when its edge is grabbed. The shrink clamp has no room
  left. The first pointer movement adds a step in the grabbed direction, without travel, and that
  commits. Rendering floors a bar at 24px and keeps its edges grabbable, so every such bar is
  reachable with a mouse. Touch needs 132px of rendered width before the edges exist. That is
  several drag steps on every scale, so touch never reaches this case.
- The progress handle is less guarded than the bar. It has no check for the primary pointer or the
  left button, and a right button press starts it. It does not filter by pointer id, and a second
  finger's movement rewrites the percentage of the running gesture.

Move, resize and progress each have a keyboard equivalent, with the same permission checks and the
same drag bounds. The key map is in [Keyboard and screen readers](accessibility). Drawing a
dependency arrow has no keyboard equivalent.
